Trends

The one thing you cannot see from inside your own week.

Act 4 answers a single question — am I getting better at this — and the answer changes with the window you ask it in. Six weeks is noise and the app says so. Six months is a trend. A year is a story, so it gets told as one.

T1 · NEW
The window decides the verdict Tap between six weeks, six months and the year. On the short window the headline reads too early to say and the rows call themselves noise — the same data, honestly labelled. This is the whole design idea of the act.
T2 · NEW
Four lines, each with its own verdict Resting pulse, variability, sleep and capacity — value now, change over the window, a sparkline, and one sentence saying whether it means anything. Amber is used for “this went the wrong way”, never for alarm.
T3 · NEW
Showing up, as a grid One dot per day: slept and worn, moved, or missing. It is the only place the app counts anything, and it counts attendance rather than achievement — the honest predictor of every other line on the screen.
T4 · NEW
Capacity — with its error bar admitted The estimate, its curve, the three things that actually moved it with weights, and one line on what would move it next. It ends by telling you it is an estimate, because it is.
T5 · NEW
Rhythm — regularity, not duration Sleep against your need over the window, then how far each week's bedtime drifted from your own hour. The bars are the argument: the flat stretch in the middle is when everything else improved.
T6 · NEW
The year, as chapters Four periods with a name and a sentence, a short list of firsts, and Svea's read on why it happened. No confetti, no medals for records you did not try to set.
What Act 4 refuses to do

No percentile against strangers, no age-group ranking, no weekly score. Every comparison on these screens is you against you, and every trend has to survive its own window before it is drawn as a trend.

Night shift, same act

Flip schedule and Rhythm stops asking about bedtime and starts asking about anchor drift — how far each week's main sleep moved from your own anchor, which is the only regularity a rotating roster can offer.

Four acts in, what is left

The night, the day, the effort and the bigger picture all exist now. What remains is plumbing rather than design: onboarding and pairing, where logged workouts live, sources and permissions, and the profile that holds your need, your zones and your anchor.
