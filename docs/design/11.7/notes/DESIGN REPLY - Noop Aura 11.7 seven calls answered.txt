2. THE CHANGE LIST — DON'T GROUP BY MONTH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  243 of 284 releases in one month is not history, it is an import artefact.
  A month grouping would draw four small groups and one enormous one, and
  the shape of that screen would tell the reader something false about how
  the app was built.

  SO: NO GROUPING. A flat list, newest first, in stored order — which you
  already have. Each release is its own block:

      11.7.0                                    September 2026
      A stress screen that keeps up, WHOOP 5 readings in the units
      the strap sends, and a ring that stops repeating itself
      · entry
      · entry

  The version is the heading and the date is a caption beside it. The
  version number is the thing that is unique, ordered and true; the date is
  context, so it is typeset as context.

  THE ONLY DIVIDER IS THE YEAR. A thin rule where the year changes, and
  nothing else. No month rules, no counts, no "243 releases in June".

  ISSUE NUMBERS AND CREDITS: both KEPT, both MOVED. This was already settled
  in the Act 10 implementation pack and it still holds — "(#2191, #2194)"
  and "(thanks @Trillient)" come OUT of the bold lead-in and go on a muted
  trailing line under the entry. Not inline, not behind a disclosure.
  Reason: inline, they interrupt the one sentence the reader wants; deleted,
  we would be erasing the only credit a contributor gets. A quiet line under
  the entry is both honest and out of the way.

  So the parsing you need is: split the trailing "(#...)" and "(thanks @...)"
  out of the lead-in, keep the rest of the bold intact. If a lead-in has
  neither, the trailing line is absent — not empty.

5. BLUED: B SHIPS, AND ITS NAME CHANGES TO NAVY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  >> B. And rename it NAVY.

  WHY B
    · It is what wearers have had since 15 June. Shipping A now would change
      the icon under everyone who chose the blue alternate, for a difference
      they did not ask for.
    · It has a generator. make_icon.py re-renders it at any size from source.
      A is a static raster. That is the same standard I set for the orb in
      item 6, and it would be incoherent to demand it there and waive it here.
    · A shipped for one day. Nobody has it set. There is no continuity to
      protect.

  WHY THE NAME CHANGES
    "Blued" describes A — brushed blued titanium. B is gold on a navy
    gradient and is not blued anything. The asset is already called
    AppIcon-Navy, so the shipped name and the shown name finally agree.

  A GOES TO THE ARCHIVE, not the bin. It is the better-looking tile of the
  two. If a brushed-metal blue is ever wanted it comes back as a rendered
  member of the family, not as that file.

  >> AND NOW THE PART THAT MATTERS MORE THAN A OR B.

  THE SOURCE ART CORRECTED MY GEOMETRY. Both candidates show the same mark,
  and it is not the one I drew. These numbers are MEASURED off
  blued-b-navy-1024.png — gold-pixel runs at the horizontal midline and an
  angular scan at the ring's mid-radius — not matched by eye:

      property        what ships                 what I had drawn
      gap centre      337° (23° CCW of top)     180°, at the foot
      gap width       79.5°                      76°
      ring outer      77.9% of the tile          74%
      stroke          13.5% of the tile          7.5%
      core            18%, at the CENTRE         no core; a dot ON the arc

  So the sweep is: conic from 17°, ink for 77.9%, gap for the rest.

  THE OPENING IS NOT ON THE VERTICAL and must not be tidied onto it. That
  23° tilt is what stops the mark reading as a symmetrical logo, and a build
  that "corrects" it to dead-top will look subtly wrong beside Navy.

  My set's own rule is "every icon is something that already exists inside
  the app". Under that rule four of my five icons failed and the only one
  that passed was the one I had not drawn. So the family is redrawn to the
  shipped geometry — gap at the top, core at the centre, heavier stroke —
  and Navy is in the sheet as the actual 1024 file, not a redrawing of it.

  This is a straight improvement, not just a correction. A core at the
  centre survives 29 pt where a dot riding a 2 px stroke does not, and the
  13.5% stroke is the real reason the shipped icon has always held at small
  sizes — my 7.5% was almost half of it.

  THE SET SHIPS FIVE: Titanium (default), Aura, Navy, Breathe, Orb.
  Titanium and Aura are drawn to the spec in the icon sheet. Navy, Breathe
  and the Orb are exports. The five numbers above are the build spec; if any
  of them still disagrees with the 1024 source, THE SOURCE WINS — tell me and
  I will correct the sheet rather than the other way round.

  ONE ADDITION SINCE THE FIRST REPLY: Cast is out and BREATHE is in — the
  today orb with the pacer's ring around it, frozen at the top of an inhale.
  It needs the same treatment as the Orb icon: rendered from the app's own

7. THE LIVE ACTIVITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Good news received: one session object already, and two of my three
  behaviours already true. Only pause and the set-counter truncation are
  work.

  >> PAUSE — YOUR READING IS CORRECT, CONFIRMED. Pause freezes the whole
     session. Whichever clock is showing holds its value; on resume it
     continues from that value. Pause during rest and rest ends later by the
     length of the pause, and the strap's five-second buzz moves with it.
     Nothing catches up, nothing is skipped.

  >> WHERE PAUSE LIVES: THE lift-live SCREEN ONLY. Not the in-app bar.
     The bar has no controls by the same rule the Live Activity has none — a
     control whose consequence you cannot see is not a control. The bar
     shows state and opens lift-live; lift-live is where the session is
     operated. So: one pause control, one screen, everywhere else reflects it.

  >> BPM: TAKE IT OUT.
     It is not in my four slots and it should not be a fifth. Two reasons.
     Mid-lift bpm is not actionable — nobody changes a set because the number
     says 138 — and the compact and minimal presentations are already at
     their ceiling with four. Adding a figure that cannot survive into two of
     the four presentations means the surfaces stop agreeing, which is the
     thing item 7 is for.
     You are right that an em dash there would contradict the numbers slot.
     That contradiction is the argument for removing it, not for inventing a
     third rule.
     IF PRODUCT OVERRULES ME: Lock Screen and expanded only, never compact or
     minimal, and with no reading the element is ABSENT — the row takes the
     width, same as the numbers slot. Never a dash.

  THE SET COUNTER MUST NOT TRUNCATE. Noted that it can today. Truncation
  order is: exercise name first and only. The clock and the set counter keep
  their width at every size — "3 of 19" losing its tail is worse than the
  exercise name losing its tail, because the reader can see which exercise
  they are doing and cannot see how many sets are left.
