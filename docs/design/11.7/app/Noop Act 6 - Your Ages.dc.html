Three ages, kept apart on purpose.

Body Age answers how is my body doing against the clock. Fitness Age answers how much oxygen can I use. The domains answer where do I stand today. Merging them into one hero number would be the easy design and the dishonest one, so the screen stacks them in descending authority instead — and every one of them carries its error where it is read.

{{ c.k }}
T1 · NEW
The band is the number Reference apps put the age in an orb and hide the error bar. Here the ± 5 years is the hero graphic: a lit span on a real age scale, with your actual age marked as a tick beside it. Younger or older is readable from position, before any colour or copy — and it makes a one-week wobble visibly meaningless.
T2 · NEW
Component one — a signed contribution Bars leave a centre line. Left in cyan takes years off, right in amber adds them, and the signed value is stated in text so colour is never the only carrier. Length is relative to the largest driver, never to the age itself. Tap any row for its own screen.
T3 · NEW
Component two — a weighted standing Deliberately the opposite shape: no centre line, no sign, left-anchored fills on a shared 0–100 ruler, in periwinkle rather than the signed pair. Weight is carried by bar thickness and stated as “28% of score”, so the heaviest domain reads first. Nobody should mistake one of these for a driver.
T4 · NEW
Uncertainty travels into the history The ten-week series is drawn inside a ribbon of the same ± 5 years. The line moves; the ribbon dwarfs it. That is the correct impression, and it costs nothing to draw.
T5 · NEW
The first week is the product The not-ready state keeps the same hero frame and puts an em dash where the number goes — so the shape of the answer is learned before the answer arrives. Four statuses, each a word plus a distinct mark: Ready, Building, Needed, Optional. It says the date the first estimate lands.
T6 · NEW
Fitness Age is demoted, not hidden Two small tiles in periwinkle under the heading “A different question”, with the caveat set as body copy directly beneath rather than behind an info tap. Different hue, smaller numbers, subordinate position — three signals that it is not the headline.
W3 · NEW
The weight trend, inline on the Weight row Direction and rate on the row itself, gated twice — the 0.5 kg noise threshold and the six-week window. Together they mean most wearers see no direction at all for six weeks, so learning is the design's normal state and says what it is waiting for rather than drawing a flat line. steady is the other quiet case: the window is long enough, the movement is not. No goal line, no target, no to go figure, no projection — and the sparkline only appears once the window gate is passed, because a six-week line invites exactly the reading the gate exists to prevent. On weightTrend.
Q8 · CALL
Health hub vs Your ages — my answer Your ages owns every estimate. The Health hub keeps only what was measured. That line is worth more than tidiness: the app already promises the wearer will always know whether they are reading a measurement or a generated sentence, and this makes the same cut for measurement versus estimate. The hub becomes vitals and composition — recorded facts, no error bars — and hands off to Your ages in a single card that shows both numbers so nobody has to hunt. Folding the ages back into the hub would put a ± 5 year model next to a resting heart rate of 52 as if they were the same kind of claim.
