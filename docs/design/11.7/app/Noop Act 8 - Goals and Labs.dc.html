You

A goal the data won't back is said to be unrealistic.

Two features that could each become a lie machine. A goal screen invites invented streaks and a lab screen invites an OCR misread of your own blood work, so both are built around the moment they could go wrong: progress is only counted from sessions actually recorded, and nothing enters the lab book until you confirm it against the strip of page it was read from. Warm amber is this act's colour, carried from You, where both live.

{{ c.k }}
T1 · NEW
The journey is a route, not a ring A ring implies closure and punishes a missed week. The route is a line with waypoints: the lit part is behind you, the dim part ahead, and a bad week simply means the lit part stops where it stopped. Nothing resets, nothing breaks.
T2 · NEW
Feasibility is a verdict, with its evidence Set a date and the call appears with the three measurements behind it — long run, weekly load, weeks available — each as a bar against what the goal needs. Try the feasibility tweak: on unrealistic the card turns clay, the commit button says commit anyway, and it names the date the data would accept instead.
T3 · NEW
Biomarkers as distance from the band One plot instead of seven sparklines: the dashed circle is the edge of the reference band, each spoke a marker, and the only thing that matters is whether its dot sits inside. Two out of range read instantly, without a table and without colour doing the work alone — every dot's row states the value and the word.
T4 · NEW
The review step, made unmistakable The risk is accepting a misread of your own blood work, so the screen opens with nothing is stored yet, shows the strip of page each value came from, marks a low-confidence read in amber, and keeps Save inert until something is confirmed. Confirm · Fix · Discard, per row, and the footer counts what will actually be kept.
T5 · NEW
A marker screen that refuses to interpret Four draws against the lab's band, what was drawn the same day, and one honest line: four points cannot support a correlation. The page ends by naming what it is not — not a diagnosis, not advice — because a lab value beside recovery data is exactly where an app is tempted to become both.
