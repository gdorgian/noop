WHAT THE CONSTANTS CORRECTED Two of my numbers, and both corrections make the mark plainer. The opening is centred at twelve o'clock, dead on the vertical, where I had it 23° counter-clockwise: the mark is symmetrical about its own axis and always was, so the tilt I had built a paragraph of reasoning around was an artefact of my scan. And the gap is narrower — cap centres 58° apart, 302° of ink, and a visible opening of 33.9° once the round caps are drawn in, against the 79.5° hole I had. What survives is the part that mattered: the reading is a core at the centre rather than a dot riding the arc, and the stroke is 13.5% of the tile, which is the real reason the shipped icon holds at 29 pt.
TWO ORBS, AND WHY THAT IS FINE Cast is out — it was a formal exercise, and it looked like one. In its place is Breathe, which earns its slot the way everything else here does: it is a real part of the app, five screens deep, and it is the only icon that opens onto something you do rather than something you read. Two orbs work because they differ on three axes at once — hue (cyan against periwinkle), shape (a true circle against a blob) and structure (a closed pace ring against a soft halo). Any one of those alone would not survive 29 pt. One cost, stated: Titanium is now the set's only pale tile. Cast was the other. A wearer on a light wallpaper has exactly one icon that holds, and it happens to be the default — workable, but it is a real narrowing and worth knowing about rather than discovering.

THE ROW Appearance · App icon settings/appearance · replaces the Later chip The row stops being a promise. It becomes what every other choice in Appearance already is — a control with the options visible and the current one obvious — except the options are surfaces, so they are shown rather than named twice. Tiles are 46 pt in a 44 pt target, three across, wrapping to a second line. The name sits under each, and the picked one carries a ring and the app's own aura blue rather than a tick: a tick on an icon reads as a badge on the icon. Tap the tiles — the row keeps its answer. Changing an alternate icon is a system alert Noop does not own, so the row says so in one line instead of letting the wearer wonder what the flash was.

FOR THE BUILD Five things to hold to
The mark is make_icon.py's, to the constant. Outer radius 0.39 of the tile, stroke 0.135, core radius 0.090. 302° of ink, round caps at half the stroke, the gap centred at twelve o'clock with cap centres 29° either side. Engineering measured the shipped 1024 against those constants and every radius came back within a pixel — that is anti-aliasing off a 4× render, not drift. Navy is that file; Titanium and Aura are the same arithmetic in other surfaces, and nothing here is a number anybody read off a picture. The stroke has a floor. 13.5% of the tile, never under 2 pt — at 29 pt in a Settings row a thin arc turns into a grey smudge, which is how an icon stops being recognisable at the one size everybody sees it. An open ring reads; a closed ring acts. The instrument's ring is open and heavy because a reading is never finished. Breathe's pace ring is closed and thin because a breath is a cycle that completes. Do not let the two converge — that distinction is the only thing keeping Breathe from reading as a fourth instrument at small sizes. Navy, Breathe and the Orb are exports, not re-drawings. Navy comes out of the repo's own generator; Breathe and the Orb render from the app's own orb code, frozen at one moment — Breathe at the top of an inhale, so the pace ring is at its widest. All three should fail a test if the shipped file stops matching its source. Titanium and Aura are drawn to the spec above. No text in any tile, at any size. Titanium stays the default. Not on contrast — it is a pale tile and it wants a dark wallpaper behind it. It is the default because it matches the hardware, and a wearer who never opens Appearance should have the icon that looks like the thing on their wrist.
