The orb paces you. Your pulse, inside it, answers.

Nothing in the orb but one number and one word. Your live heart rate sits dead centre — no unit, nothing beside it — and the instruction reads underneath: breathe in, hold, breathe out, hold. The number rises on the inhale and falls on the exhale exactly as the physiology says it should. No title, no counter, no session to start.

W3 · NEW
What today has cost — a card, and day/energy behind it Spend only, and the card says four things in reading order: what it measured, the split, where the day is heading as a range, and how much of the day it saw — in words, never as a percentage chip. Five designed readings off the engine's enums, on energyCase, not twenty combinations. The thin-number answer here is the interval, not withholding: the band widens as coverage falls, so steps shows a range where a measured day shows a figure, and only cold has no measured spend at all — there the one figure Noop can always stand behind is stated flat, with the chip carrying the reason. On day/energy the coverage gap is a hairline column, the same idea as the gauge's ghost ticks: a gap is not a zero. A past day loses the projection entirely, because a forecast for yesterday is not a forecast.
W1b · NEW
What's new, in Noop's language instead of the merge's day/release, the sheet a version bump presents once. Four readings, written as facts about the app rather than features: the lift, what the day cost, what changed, speaking to Svea. Two states — bump carries the New chip and Start using it; bell drops both, because a thing you chose to open is not news. Two doors: the bell's Worth knowing row, and Settings → About. The per-version list is set at length and deliberately unwritten — the release feed owns those strings.
T1 · NEW
Today Orb, body state, last night in one line, the day's shape, then Heart · Vitals · Stress. The Heart card carries the live number itself, so the pulse is on the home screen without a screen of its own. The Charge gauge around the orb now behaves like a battery: the ghost ticks are the headroom you woke with and the lit arc is what is left. No numbers under the orb — the figure is in the sentence, and tapping the orb opens Where it went, which prices every drain: hours awake, the intervals, the stressed minutes, the steps over normal. The instruction inside the orb is still one word, in · hold · out · hold, in step with the orb's own swell.
T3 · NEW
Heart Replaces the full-screen live face: live bpm and beat-to-beat at the top, today's curve, where the day was spent by zone, then resting rate, variability and recovery. A room, not a wall.
T3
Vitals — and what out of zone looks like Every signal carries its own normal zone as shaded track, its baseline as a pale tick, the range ends in figures, and the difference as a chip. Breathing rate is deliberately outside its zone here: the figure, the chip, the dot and the card edge all turn amber — one colour, four places, no alarm. The count at the top and the Vitals row on Today change with it.
T5 · NEW
The + button is for logging Coffee, water, a meal, a drink, intimacy, a nap — one tap each, counts on the tile, and it all lands in tonight's read. Starting a workout belongs to Act 3, not here.
Is Today finished?

One thing is still missing, and it belongs to Act 3: today's session — the one line that says whether to train and how hard, with accept or swap. That is the last card Today needs. Everything else it could hold — streaks, rings, a score — it is better without.

Night shift, same act

Flip schedule and “the day so far” becomes the span from your 15:22 waking to now, the greeting stops saying morning, and the timeline shows a shift rather than a commute. The word day means your day, wherever it lands on a clock.
