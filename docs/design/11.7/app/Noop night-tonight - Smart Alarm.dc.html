Before launch · item 4 Act 1 · night/tonight

Smart Alarm

Smaller than it looked. night/tonight already has the smart-wake row and its toggle. Two things are missing, and both are the difference between a switch and an alarm you can rely on.

1 · There is no way to set the time

The row reads Wake me between 06:10 and 06:40 and Act 1 says “before your 06:25 alarm” in four places — but 06:25 comes from nowhere the user can reach. A person who wakes to a wrist buzz today would lose their alarm and could not set a new one.

2 · The best thing about it is unsaid

It arms the strap’s own firmware alarm. It fires with the phone off, asleep, silenced or in another room. That is a sentence a phone alarm cannot say, and the screen never says it — so it also never says when it will not fire.

Why two rows and not one

A row carrying both a chevron and a toggle is ambiguous every time. So setting and arming are separate rows: the first opens the screen, the second is the switch. When the switch is off, the first row’s time drops to 38 % and the sub reads Not armed — the setting survives, the alarm does not.

The implementation trap

06:25 is currently a literal in four places. Act 1’s TERMS.alarm feeds the tonight teaser, the bedtime sub-line, the Lights out by length and the night-shift variant at 15:25. Making the time settable means all four read one value — and the night-shift schedule has to follow it, which is the same promise step 2 of onboarding makes.

Why the failures get a card

An alarm is the one feature where a silent failure costs someone their morning. The app already has the convention — every switch here says what it costs you in battery — and this is the same instinct pointed at reliability. Three plain lines, before arming, not a warning after the fact.

Never

No snooze count, no wake-up score, no you woke in deep sleep verdict the morning after. The alarm’s job ends when you are awake. And no phone-side fallback alarm that fires alongside it — two alarms for one wake is how people stop trusting both.
