Before launch · item 3 Act 2 · the fifth screen

Breathe

The one thing Noop does that a phone cannot. The engine, the eighteen-protocol catalog and the haptics are written and switched off; the orb on today paces nothing, buzzes nothing and measures nothing. The orb becomes the door.

Scoped as the player plus the resonance sweep, because the sweep is the feature and not a later mode. It is the only piece in the pack that is an activity rather than a display, and the loop between the cue, the body and the graphic is the whole of it.

The hue, settled

No new token. Breathe’s hue is the orb — the app’s one permitted gradient, used here at full size on the one screen where the orb is the subject rather than a card. Everything else on the screen is neutral so the orb is the brightest thing in the room.

The line the copy cannot cross

Never therapy, never treatment, never a claim about anxiety, blood pressure or recovery. It is a pace that suits this person’s physiology — that is the whole promise, and it is enough.

The two things that make this the flagship

The strap buzzes the cue. Not a chime, not a flashing ring — a wrist tap on each transition, so the session works with the eyes shut and the phone face down. That is the sentence a phone app cannot say, and it should be visible in the header while it is happening.

The trace is live and it is the point. The dashed pacer and the bright heart line on one axis is the loop between cue, body and graphic. If the trace is faked, smoothed into a sine, or drawn after the fact, the feature is a timer with a gradient.

Never

No therapy or treatment framing, anywhere, in any state — not for anxiety, blood pressure, recovery or sleep. No streak, no badge, no minutes-per-week goal: What Noop will not ask you covers this screen too. And the four intense protocols carry a plain seated-only line rather than a modal warning, because a modal on a breathing screen is the wrong register.
