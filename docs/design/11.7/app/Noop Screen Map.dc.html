01

Four questions, in the order you ask them

Not four features — four questions a person actually has, ranked. Every screen in this document exists to answer one of them, and a screen that answers none is plumbing. Sleep leads because it's the one thing you can change tonight and feel tomorrow.

1

“How did I sleep — and what should I do about tonight?”

Answered by Rest, which stops being a report card and becomes a two-ended thing: last night explained, tonight decided.

2

“What is my body doing today — and is that normal for me?”

Answered by Today. The missing piece is shape — the body isn't one value at 8am, it's an arc from wake to now.

3

“Can I train today — and what did that session cost me?”

Answered by Effort. Third on purpose: training is the thing you spend, and you can't spend what the first two questions haven't earned.

Answered by Trends. The one thing a person cannot see from inside their own week — and the only honest reason to keep wearing the strap for a year.

02

Joy, not vigilance

The same data can make someone curious or make them anxious, and that's entirely a presentation decision. These seven rules decide it, and they apply to every screen in section 04 without exception.

01

You are the only benchmark

Every value reads against your own band, never a population percentile. No leaderboards, no “below average for your age”.

02

Nothing goes red at you

Depleted is warm, not alarming. Red is reserved for the strap being dead — a hardware fact, not a verdict on your body.

03

Every number arrives with a verb

If we can't say what to do differently, the number is trivia. Trivia goes one level deeper, where you went looking for it.

04

A bad night gets a cause, not a score

“You went to bed at 1:40 and drank” is useful. “62” is a thing to worry about at 3am. Same data, opposite effect.

05

No streaks, no rings to close

Nothing that punishes a rest day, a night out, or a week off. Recovery apps that gamify compliance manufacture the anxiety they treat.

06

Precision we don't have is a lie

84%, not 84.3%. A range when confidence is low, and the words “estimate” and “approximation” where they're true.

07

The app is allowed to say nothing

An ordinary day gets one line and silence. Not every day needs a finding, and pretending otherwise is how a dashboard becomes noise.

Together these are the difference between “here is your body” and “here is what your body needs from you”. Same numbers. Only the second one is worth opening.

Everything the app ships, in eight groups

Sleep — the centre

3 build · 4 fold

The app's real subject. Two of these don't exist in Noop today — they're the ones that turn a sleep report into a sleep decision.

  • Tonight NEWthe evening screen — when to go to bed, what to avoid, alarm set. The only screen that changes tomorrow
  • Why last night NEWthe cause, not the score — bedtime, alcohol, late meal, room temp, lined up against the night

App & control

3 build · 6 fold

06

Four acts, then the plumbing

One act per question, in the order from section 01. Each act is a complete, demoable answer on its own — you could stop after any of them and still have shipped something coherent, which is the opposite of how the current app grew.

ACT 1

The night

Rest · Tonight · Why last night · Sleep debt · JournalAnswers question 1 end to end. Tonight and the journal are the same loop seen from both ends — you log the day, and the app tells you what time to close it. Rest gains past-night swiping and the expandable hypnogram in the same pass.
4
ACT 2

The day

Today · The day so far · Vitals · Stress · HeartAnswers question 2. The day-so-far timeline is the piece Noop is missing entirely: your body as an arc from wake to now, not a number stamped at 8am. Quick actions ships here because it is how you reach the live screens.
5
ACT 3

The effort

Today's session · Choose something else · Ready · In the effort · Intervals · Session detailAnswers question 3. Breathing moved out of this act and into the orb on Act 2's home screen — it is the one control you have over the nervous system, so it belongs where you already are, not behind a start button.
6
ACT 4

The bigger picture

Trends · Capacity · Rhythm · The year so farAnswers question 4, and it is the act that earns the year of wear. Four screens rather than six: the dossier, the weekly letter and the scoring page all folded into the three trend rooms. Everything here is retrospective and calm by construction — no live data, no alerts, nothing that can interrupt you.
4
THEN

The plumbing

You · Zones · Everything you logged · Your strap · Data and permissions · Settings · Onboarding · PairingNecessary, not the point. Eight screens rather than twelve — sources, backup, automations, compare and the lab book all folded into Data and Settings. Deliberately last so onboarding can promise four acts that already exist.
8

Two tests hold the whole plan together. A screen only exists if you'd go looking for it — that's what turns twenty-six More-tab rows into four rooms. And a screen only ships if it answers one of the four questions — that's what keeps it from growing back.
